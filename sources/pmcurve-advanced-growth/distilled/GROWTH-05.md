# Resurrection & Reactivation (GROWTH-05)

**Competency:** Product Growth
**Coverage:** Strong

## Definition
Strategies for winning back churned users, including campaign design, timing optimization, and creating compelling return experiences.

## Sources

### 03_Retention_and_Resurrection (Adv_Growth_PM/)
- **Relevance:** High
- **Key Excerpt:** "To work on resurrections, we have to go back to the mindsets which led to voluntary churn: Pissed, Disappointed, Better elsewhere, Similar offering elsewhere. Repeating the same lines about the product isn't going to work because it doesn't get their attention or curiosity."
- **Concepts:** Churn Mindsets, Resurrection Campaigns, Novelty Requirement

### 03_Retention_and_Resurrection (Adv_Growth_PM/)
- **Relevance:** High
- **Key Excerpt:** "The hidden cost of resurrection campaigns lies in channel effectiveness and brand reputation. Email channel effectiveness can be reduced when we contact high volume of users and most don't respond."
- **Concepts:** Campaign Hidden Costs, Channel Effectiveness, Spam Risk

## Key Frameworks

### Churn Classification
| Type | Definition | Resurrection Probability |
|------|------------|-------------------------|
| Voluntary | User chose to leave | Higher (addressable reasons) |
| Involuntary | Couldn't use product | Lower (circumstances) |

### Voluntary Churn Stages
| Stage | Resurrection Chance | Priority |
|-------|---------------------|----------|
| Before activation | Lowest | Low |
| Habit building stage | Medium | High |
| Engaged stage | Highest | High |

**Key Insight:** Start with users who churned after building habits - they experienced core value.

### Resurrection Timing
**Optimal Window Analysis:**
1. Plot probability of return vs inactivity period
2. Identify inflection point where probability drops
3. Start campaigns just before significant drop
4. Use natural frequency intervals for x-axis

**Example:** If return probability drops significantly after 3 weeks → start campaigns at week 3

### Hidden Costs of Bad Campaigns
1. **Email Domain Reputation:** High spam/unsubscribe rates hurt deliverability
2. **Brand Reputation:** Pushy campaigns = negative word of mouth
3. **Future Prospects:** Damaged reputation affects new user acquisition

### Resurrection Campaign Elements
**Novelty Sources:**
- New features addressing why they left
- New use cases that may appeal
- Incentives (discounts, special offers)
- Contextual timing (New Year, seasonal)

**5-Step Process:**
1. Find out why user went dormant
2. Craft message addressing that reason
3. Determine right intervention timing
4. Select appropriate delivery channel
5. Re-activate with clear value proposition

### Churn Mindset → Response Matrix
| Mindset | Cause | Effective Response |
|---------|-------|-------------------|
| Pissed | Product failed promise | Apology + incentive |
| Disappointed | Didn't meet need | New features addressing gap |
| Better elsewhere | Competitor won | Unique differentiator |
| Similar elsewhere | Equivalent options | Marketing/pricing move |

### Re-engagement Experience Design
**Farmville Example:**
- Problem: Returning to a dead farm is demoralizing
- Solution: One-click cleanup + specific re-engagement path
- Learning: Design tailored return experiences, not just notifications

## Proof of Work

### Doing It (Work Context)
- Designing resurrection campaigns matched to churn mindset (pissed, disappointed, better elsewhere, similar elsewhere)
- Timing re-engagement before the probability-of-return inflection point
- Avoiding hidden costs: email domain reputation damage, brand reputation erosion

### Demonstrating It (Interview/Review Context)
- Growth interviews may test winback thinking — show you understand churn mindsets and tailored responses
- Key principle: "Repeating the same product lines won't work — novelty is required to re-capture attention"
- Reference 5-step process: find why they left → craft message → time it right → choose channel → re-activate
- *Practice: any retention/churn scenario — always consider the resurrection angle*
