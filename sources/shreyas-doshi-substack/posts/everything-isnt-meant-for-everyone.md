# Everything isn‘t meant for everyone.

*As you create (and consume) in 2026, keep this simple but often overlooked principle in mind.*

One assumption many people implicitly make in today’s world is that everything is supposed to be meant for everyone. And if that isn’t the case, that’s a bug that must be fixed.

But you must discard this assumption.

This advice is useful if you’re consuming said thing, but profound if you’re producing said thing.

The visceral understanding that the products or services you create are not meant for everyone and the insight of who it is that you are targeting (and why) will unleash creativity and differentiation that you’ve observed in every iconic product and brand that has moved you and made a difference in your life.

Yes, at some point you must “scale”, but when that point comes, it will be obvious.

Until then, targeting everyone and hoping to please everyone will very likely please no one.

This is also why “feedback”, while useful, can also lead you astray.

People will give you all kinds of feedback for all sorts of things. People love giving feedback. In fact, the smarter your target audience, the more unsolicited feedback you’ll get.

Most of that feedback should be reviewed, evaluated, added to your internal model, and then discarded1.

Because acting on as much feedback as you can, while it might make you feel productive (and perhaps even virtuous), will almost certainly lead to a watered down product that over time loses resonance with your audience.
