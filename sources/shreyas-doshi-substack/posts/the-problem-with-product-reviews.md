# The Problem with Product Reviews

*Most product reviews offer low ROI because the quality of the discussion is often mediocre. Plus, the managers presenting at product reviews are looking for a green light, not real feedback.*

One problem with most product reviews is that feedback tends to be incremental like _“this is fine, that is great, great document, change this/that & you’re green lighted”,_ instead of takeaways such as:

  * this problem isn’t important

  * there is no differentiation here

  * we can build it, but can’t sell it




Obviously, not every product review needs to be like this. 

The important observation is that **barely any product review is like this**. 

It might not feel good to the team in the moment, but it’s the kind of insightful feedback teams need to get before they waste months/years of their life.

In top tier companies, this can happen at lower levels: before the big product review with the CEO, the VP does his/her own reviews/discussions with the team, guiding them in the right direction, and sharing this type of feedback. 

But this still remains a fairly rare skill among VPs, GMs, and CPOs. 

In most companies, the **Bias-for-Building** fallacy is irresistible. With Bias-for-Building, we don’t take the time to thoroughly understand the problem, domain, competitors and other determinants of product success, because **we feel compelled to be in constant motion**. We tell ourselves we must **begin building** and just iterate based on feedback, for everything.

Why does this happen? Let’s set ideal-world theory aside. 

The reality is that when companies say they “move fast”, in practice, they are primarily optimizing for _time to begin writing the first line of code_ , and they are almost never optimizing for _time to maximum impact_1.

[](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!fWDy!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa9733201-adac-407c-891a-420b3cb7910c_1282x1020.png)

So, in practice, product reviews just become an inconvenient but necessary hurdle in the race for more resources, better perf ratings, and a quick green light, all in the name of moving fast.

Finally, note that these are “first-world problems” i.e. problems in Tier 1 companies and fast-growing Tier 2 companies.

Tier 3 / Tier 4 companies have the opposite problem: **too much analysis, too much gatekeeping, not enough committed action**.

But we need real product insight and clarity everywhere.
