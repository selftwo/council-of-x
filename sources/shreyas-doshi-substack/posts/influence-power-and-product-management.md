# Influence, Power, and Product Management in Modern Product Companies

In modern tier 1 software product companies1, if a PM complains that the **#1** challenge for PMs is that product management does not have enough influence on product decisions, odds are high that this PM does not possess 

A) the Product Sense necessary for the role they are in, or 

B) the ability to persuade very capable peers

**Note: This does not mean that this is always the case, but it is more often the case than not.**

Oftentimes, such a PM will speak for the entire PM org and will find some other kindred souls who agree, when the truth is that there are good reasons for this situation where product management doesn’t have all the power and influence.

The alternative — where such PMs are given the extremely high degree of influence they seek — usually leads to worse outcomes for the product and the company.

Alas, many managers of PMs don’t have the maturity to identify the real issue, and many others don’t have the courage or skills to communicate the real issue to such PMs.

So these PM managers dutifully note the feedback, commiserate, promise to do something about the situation, write a “roles & responsibilities of PM / Eng / Design”, ship swimlanes docs, institute some process change or other, etc. etc. 

**None of which solve the problem because there is no problem.**

Fact is that most PMs will always seek more influence / control / power than they happen to have currently. Companies that give product management a bunch of power, telling PMs “you’re top dog and you call the shots” end up shipping lower quality and less innovative products over time. These companies might still appear to be ‘doing well’ for a few years after they’ve handed over the reins to product management, but that is more due to [legacy momentum](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1348093583300022275?s=20) than due to product management being the dominant function at the company. 

**What is the solution?**

First, middle management and senior management within the PM org should clearly call out that a PM-dominated culture is not the goal. This is tough to do in practice because many managers want to be liked by their IC PMs, and in these cultures, they are also being evaluated by their ICs in performance reviews. But just because it’s tough doesn’t mean it isn’t right. Why else are Directors and VPs of Product Management getting paid the very big bucks they get paid? To do easy things and avoid tough things?

Second, if you’re a founder / CEO of a company that is scaling rapidly, you must set the tone. Assuming you see the wisdom and rationale behind it, you must be as clear as possible that a product team where PMs are calling all the shots is not the culture you want at your company.

Now, if you’re an IC PM or a first-level manager of PMs at a company that has a high hiring bar across the board, consider perhaps that the only way to build highly successful products is when everyone working on the product is very vested in its success. Consider then that this singular focus on success is impossible if one specific role insists on having more power than the other roles.

**How is this changing with AI?**

It gets even more important to internalize all of this, particularly the need for stronger Product Sense across all functions (i.e. not just PM, but eng, design, data science, research, etc.) and the ability to influence up, down, and across the company.

I’ve seen a major shift over the past 6 months in people’s recognition that ultimately the most important product skill is neither coordination nor “running the eng work”, but the ability to make more correct product decisions, macro and micro, in the presence of ambiguity and with the luxury to build many more prototypes and experiments.

While this shift is good to see, I also like to point this out: Product Sense always has been the most important product skill over the past 30-40 years :)
