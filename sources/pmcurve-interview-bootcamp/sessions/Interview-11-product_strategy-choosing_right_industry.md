# Choosing the Right Industry and Strategy

**Product Strategy Module**

In this video, **Deepak** dives into how to address strategic problems, specifically focusing on assessing the "Right Industry".

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Where to Play vs. How to Win
*   **Context**: Addressed a strategic problem (investing a large sum in a new product line).
*   **Challenge**: Requires answering "where to play" and "how to win", emphasizing that the **latter ("how to win") is more challenging**.

### 2. Assessing the Right Industry
Outlined three core questions for assessing the Right Industry:
1.  **Defining Boundaries**: Using Jobs to be Done (JTBD).
2.  **Industry Attractiveness**: Using Porter's Five Forces.
3.  **Existential Threat**: Assessing defensive necessity.

### 3. Industry Boundaries (JTBD)
*   Illustrated how to define industry boundaries using **Jobs to be Done (JTBD)**.
*   *Example*: An app like Instagram actually establishes two distinct industries: **social graph** and **graph-less network/Reels**.

### 4. Porter's Five Forces
Introduced **Porter's Five Forces** as the primary framework for assessing industry attractiveness (profitability):
1.  **Threat of New Entrants**
2.  **Buyer Power**
3.  **Threat of Substitutes**
4.  **Rivalry**
5.  **Supplier Power**

### 5. Application: Quick Commerce in India
*   Demonstrated a **nuanced application** of the Five Forces to Quick Commerce.
*   *Analysis*: Factors like **low supplier power** (leading to ad revenue/commissions) and **high growth** make the industry more attractive than a surface-level analysis might suggest.

### 6. Existential Threat
*   Explained the concept of **Existential Threat**.
*   *Example*: Flipkart entering Quick Commerce.
*   **Strategy**: A company may enter an unattractive market simply to **defend its core business** against adjacent competition.
