# Choosing the Right Metrics for Product Success

**Analytical & Growth Module**

In this video, **Deepak** dives deeper into metric selection, providing concrete examples of guardrails, North Star Metrics adjustments, and the actor-based goal breakdown.

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Guardrails vs. North Star Metrics
*   **Guardrail Example**: "Daily Usage Limit" for Instagram Reels.
    *   *Purpose*: Sets a boundary to prevent harmful, addictive effects, protecting user well-being and platform reputation.
*   **Bad NSM Example**: "Total Time Spent" for Zoom.
    *   *Flaw*: It is too far removed from product changes, easily gamified, and influenced by non-product factors (meetings are mandatory). It is not **sensitive** enough.

### 2. Criteria for Selecting Metrics
Detailed comprehensive criteria for effective metrics:
1.  **Measurable**: Can be accurately tracked.
2.  **Sensitive**: Moves based on product changes.
3.  **Hard to Game**: Reflects genuine value, not optimization hacks.
4.  **Strategic Alignment**: Ties to long-term strategy.
5.  **Practical Meaning**: Clear to understand (avoiding complex composites).

### 3. Metric Selection Process
*   **Method**: Break down goals by **Actor** (e.g., for B2B Sales: Salesperson, Company, End User).
*   **Goal**: Find metrics that are **simple, direct, and actionable** for each actor.

### 4. Context & Product Purpose
*   **Different Goals**: Distinction between **Business Goals** (e.g., Conversion) and **User Goals**.
*   **Product Purpose**: You must accurately define the product's role.
    *   *Example*: Mutual funds in ET Money may be a **retention tool**, not a direct monetization tool, effectively changing the primary success metric.
