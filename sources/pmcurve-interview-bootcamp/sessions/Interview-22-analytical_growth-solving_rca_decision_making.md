# Solving RCA and Decision-Making Questions Effectively

**Analytical & Growth Module**

In this video, **Deepak** expands on Analytical Interviews, detailing the C.H.A.S.A. framework for Root Cause Analysis (RCA) and structuring decision-making questions.

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Root Cause Analysis (RCA)
*   **Complexity**: Noted that "growth RCAs" (e.g., retention decline) are more complex than simple metric dips.
*   **C.H.A.S.A. Framework**:
    *   **C**larify Context
    *   **H**ypotheses (Formulate)
    *   **A**ccept/Reject (Test)
    *   **S**hort-term Solutions
    *   **L**ong-term Solutions (Policies)
    *   *(Note: The acronym seems to be CHASA but implies Short/Long term actions)*.
*   **Hypothesis Generation**: Examine **Internal Factors** (measurement faults, product/infra changes) vs. **External Factors** (competition, geography, traffic sources).

### 2. Decision-Making & Trade-offs
*   **Structure**:
    1.  **Goal**: Start with the decision's goal.
    2.  **Stakeholders**: Identify all affected parties.
    3.  **Metrics**: Define Success Metrics and **Counter Metrics**.
    4.  **Trade-offs**: Discuss risks to evaluate acceptability.

### 3. Metric Mathematics & Pitfalls
*   **Ratio/Composite Metrics**: Reiterated the danger.
    *   *Example*: **Conversion Rate** can dip even if total conversions increase (if traffic increases disproportionately).
    *   *Advice*: Prefer clear, directly influenceable metrics.

### 4. Resolving Ambiguity
*   **Guidance**: When metric selection is ambiguous, articulate the **product's purpose with respect to the company**.
    *   *Example*: A mutual fund product's goal for ET Money is **user retention**, not direct revenue, which guides the choice of success metric.
