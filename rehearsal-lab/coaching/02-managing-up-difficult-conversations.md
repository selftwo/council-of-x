# Managing up & difficult conversations

Sources: `podcasts/alisa-cohn.md`, `podcasts/dr-becky-kennedy.md`, `podcasts/anneka-gupta.md`, `newsletters/managing-up.md`, `newsletters/communicating-bad-news---issue-26.md`, `newsletters/this-week-7-effectively-communicating-about-a-failure-to-execs-managing-founder.md`, `newsletters/how-to-communicate-tradeoffs-so-leaders-will-listen.md`.

## Alisa Cohn (executive coach) — the script library

1. **Neutral performance opener.** Calm framing sentence, cite what you're hearing from multiple sources, invoke shared knowledge, name the outcome the conversation must end with. "It's Tuesday. We got to have this conversation. I'm not mad." Script: "What I'm hearing from them is that you're missing deadlines... we both know the most important way you can be successful here... I want to hear what you have to say, but the most important thing is that we leave this discussion knowing how you're going to keep your peers in the loop." → *Opens calm, attributes to observables, states the desired end state.*
2. **Observation-based feedback when it's only your perception.** "What I've observed is..." + concrete examples + models to copy + offer of help + why it benefits them. Pre-work test: "what's my evidence this is happening?" → *"What I've observed" + evidence, never character judgment; offers a model.*
3. **The pause script for defensiveness.** Don't argue back. "Let's pause for a second... I'm telling you this to make you better... my observation is that you're getting a little bit emotional. Can we continue now or do we need to pause? At the end of the day we really have to have this conversation." → *Pauses and restates intent instead of arguing point-by-point.*
4. **Close circular disagreement.** Mirror their reasons, name the disagreement, set a revisit date, state the decision stands: "You and I have a different point of view. Let's talk again in a week. But I want you to know this is a decision I've made." → *Mirrors first; schedules the revisit rather than repeating the argument.*
5. **Bad news first, reasons, then hope.** Deliver the decision in the first sentences, numbered reasons, then a forward commitment. → *Disappointing decision within two sentences; ends with a concrete commitment.*
6. **The crystal-clear warning.** "Have you been crystal clear about what you need?" — hedged answer means no. Name the pattern, the deadline, the consequence, and belief in them: "I need you to fix this within 30 days. Otherwise... we're going to have to part ways. I know you have it in you to change." → *Explicit deadline + explicit consequence, not vague urgency.*
7. **Specific praise builds the reservoir** that lets hard feedback land later. "I saw the way you ran that launch... all these benefits came from it... keep doing that." → *Praise = behavior + impact + "keep doing that."*
8. **Three questions to end every meeting**: What did we decide? Who does what by when? Who else needs to know? Anyone can start the ritual. → *Asks all three before the meeting ends.*

## Dr. Becky Kennedy (Good Inside)

9. **Repair after a bad moment.** "Earlier in the meeting I totally cut you off... I did disagree, but it's no excuse to talk to you that way. I'm sorry. I'm going to work on that." Repair, not perfection, builds trust ("perfect is creepy"). → *Unprompted apology owning the behavior without retracting the substance.*
10. **Connect before correct.** Join their reality first, agenda-free, even 30 seconds: "You look like you're just settling into that book. Can we get on top of this together? I know we're on the same team." → *Acknowledges their situation before the ask.*
11. **Same team + behavior/identity split.** Take identity off the table, then treat the behavior as a shared puzzle: "I know you're a good person... it's been happening consistently, which tells me something is going on that I want to get to the bottom of with you. Tell me what's been going on." → *Affirms the person, then asks an open diagnostic question instead of assigning motive.*
12. **Most Generous Interpretation first.** "Something happens in meetings where I wonder if you don't always feel heard. And then you keep talking, the room gets annoyed, we tune you out more... we're in a bad cycle. Can we work on changing this?" → *Voices a generous hypothesis and frames a mutual cycle.*
13. **Name your intention up front.** "I want to go through this in detail because I want to get ahead of some things — I think that'll help you do your most independent work." → *States intent before the threatening act.*
14. **Sturdy leadership: validate the reaction, hold the decision.** Neither "stop complaining" nor opening the cockpit door: "The way you're feeling makes sense. And here's why we're doing it... I have conviction this is the next thing we're going to experiment with." Honest-uncertainty version: "Do I have complete conviction? No. But I've put it all together and this is what we're going with. We'll reassess in a month." → *Validates feelings AND restates the decision in the same breath.*
15. **"I believe you" + "I believe in you."** Validate that it IS hard, then express confidence, and keep the task with them: "It makes sense you're nervous... I'd be nervous if you weren't. And we know you can figure this out." → *Pairs validation with confidence; doesn't take the work away.*
16. **Boundaries are what YOU will do.** "My colleague doesn't respect my boundaries" usually means you made a request. A boundary requires them to do nothing. Protest means it's working. → *Phrases limits as first-person actions.*
17. **The reverse-360.** "If I could do one thing different this week to be a better manager to you, what would it be?" — receive with only "thank you for telling me." → *Invites specific upward feedback; responds with acknowledgment, not defense.*

## Anneka Gupta

18. **"I care about you" said out loud + perception framing.** "I'm giving you this feedback because I want you to reach the pinnacle of what I know you can accomplish"; frame as "this is how you're being perceived, maybe not what you intend" — moves the talk to changing the perception. → *Explicit care statement + perception framing + 2-3 corrective actions.*
19. **Receiving hard feedback: feel it, don't react, return curious.** Let the defensive wave pass (hours/days), then ask where it came from. → *No in-the-moment rebuttal; curious follow-up later.*
20. **Difficult colleagues: motives, allies, hills.** "I can work with anyone." Find what they care about (ask people who work well with them), connect your ask to it. With a founder's pet idea: ask what objective they're really after, then "maybe instead of option A, we should explore these three others" toward that same objective. → *Articulates the objective behind the idea before opposing it.*
21. **Summarize-and-check steering.** "Let me pause and capture what's been said... is everyone in agreement or is there dissent about whether that's accurate?" Seed hypotheses, not verdicts, so seniority doesn't kill debate. → *Summarizes aloud and explicitly invites disagreement.*
22. **"Strategic" = simple why + change agent.** One-sentence why for any proposal; when told "we tried that before," ask what happened and why before re-pitching. → *States the one-sentence why; asks for history.*

## Lenny Rachitsky & Tara Seshan (newsletters)

23. **State of the Me.** Weekly email: current priorities, things on my mind, blockers I need help with. Judged on results vs. expectations, so set them explicitly. → *When accepting new work, names what slips and asks the boss to confirm.*
24. **Communicating a failed project.** Get ahead of it; context first (why it was green-lit, the hypothesis) then results; crystal-clear POV on next steps — "be the solution to the problem, not just the messenger of it." Phrasing: "This project failed. Let me tell you what happened, what we've learned, and what we're recommending." → *Names the failure plainly up front; happened → learned → recommend.*
25. **Changing a founder's mind.** Bring them on the journey as data arrives; test their hypotheses one by one ("what would convince you this isn't right?"); let them save face: "You made the right decision given what you knew; now that we know more, it makes sense to change direction." → *Frames reversal as evolution on new information.*
26. **Tradeoffs as forced either/or in company terms** (Tara Seshan). Weak: "we're thin on resources" (invites "do both"). Strong: "To build the feature we'd push single sign-on a quarter, so these 10 clients won't onboard. I don't think it's worth it for the company." Keep a visible ongoing stack rank; "repetition doesn't spoil the prayer." → *Names the specific cut and its company-level cost.*
27. **Steelman the exec's request first** (via Ami Vora; Bezos "?" story). Assume they know something you don't. "That's interesting and new to me — let's watch the customer call together." → *Curious clarifying question + strongest case FOR before arguing against.*
28. **BLUF + gavel.** Decision at top of the doc; "I'd like to gavel on this at the end of the meeting — the default is we move forward"; close with a decision or dated next steps, never an open end. → *States recommendation, default, and deadline at the start.*
