# Speaking under pressure & concision

Mined 2026-07-18 from the Lenny vault. Sources: `podcasts/matt-abrahams.md`, `podcasts/tristan-de-montebello.md`, `podcasts/paige-costello.md`, `podcasts/wes-kao.md`. Each item: mechanics → coachable behavior (transcript-checkable).

## Matt Abrahams (Think Faster, Talk Smarter)

1. **Visualization as desensitization.** 5-minute pre-event visualization: breathing, then picture the whole arc including the ending; get a photo of the room, know who'll be there. "Like a pilot in a flight simulator... it gives you a sense of agency." → *Coachable: reports having mentally rehearsed the scene, not just content.*
2. **Dare to be dull.** Self-judging every utterance against perfection eats cognitive bandwidth "like too many open apps." Just do the task: answer the question. "Strive for connection over perfection by daring to be dull." → *Starts answering directly; no perfection-stalling or restarts.*
3. **Reframe anxiety as excitement** (via Alison Wood Brooks). One arousal response, two labels. Say "this is exciting, I get to share my view." → *Pre-talk self-talk uses "excited," not "nervous."*
4. **Mantras replace negative self-talk.** A short believable counter said minutes before: "I have value to bring," "Last time this went well," "It's not about me, it's about my content." → *Has a named mantra and uses it.*
5. **Turn presentation into conversation.** Open with "Today I have three questions I'd like to answer. Question one is..." — you're now conversing, which lowers anxiety. → *Opens monologues with posed questions.*
6. **Distract the audience through minute one** (peak nerves): open with a question, story, video, artifact. → *Opening includes an audience-directed move.*
7. **Double-exhale breathing.** Belly breath, exhale twice the inhale (3 in, 6 out). "The magic of the relaxation happens during the exhale"; the voice is a wind instrument. → *Visible slow breath/pause before the first sentence under pressure.*
8. **Tongue twisters to warm up.** "We can't go from silence to brilliance without warming up." → *Warms up vocally before hard reps.*
9. **Prepare to be spontaneous.** Spontaneity is trainable: repetition, reflection, outside feedback ("we are not the best judges of our communication"). Toastmasters table topics, improv. → *Schedules spontaneous reps and reviews them.*
10. **Structure halves your burden.** Under pressure people spew lists and "take the audience on the journey of our own discovery." A structure decides HOW so you only decide WHAT — "it's like having a recipe." Pick 1-2 defaults in advance. → *Spontaneous answers have a beginning-middle-end shape, not a list.*
11. **PREP**: Point → Reason → Example → Point restated. Best for making a single point in a debate. → *Point in sentence one, restated in the last sentence.*
12. **What / So what / Now what** — his favorite for updates and feedback. Content, why it matters to this audience, what's next. Drill: after any podcast/article, 30 seconds of the three. → *Updates contain all three parts explicitly.*
13. **ADD for Q&A**: Answer cleanly first, one Detailed example, Describe relevance — "we need to actually connect those dots for our audience." Don't say "good question" reflexively; don't end with "does that make sense?" — ask "Did I answer your question?" → *Answers the asked question in sentence one, one example, explicit relevance.*
14. **Q&A as opportunity, not threat.** Let the question finish; don't interrupt or over-validate. → *No interrupting, no reflexive flattery.*
15. **WHAT for toasts**: Why we're here, How you're connected, Anecdote (accessible, where the emotion lives — "it's one thing to say 'I am so sad,' another to tell a story that is sad"), Thanks. "Nobody has ever complained about a toast that's too brief." → *All four beats, stops promptly.*
16. **4 I's feedback**: Information, Impact, Invitation, Implications. "This is the third time you've shown up late [info]. I feel you're not prioritizing this meeting [impact]. What can we do... [invitation] Because if you do, we'll finish on time [implications]." → *Feedback names event, effect, and ends with a collaborative question.*
17. **AAA apology**: Acknowledge the act itself (not "sorry you feel bad"), Appreciate the difficulty caused, Amends with a specific remedy. → *Apology names the behavior and a concrete future fix.*
18. **Normalize the anxiety.** It's the innate human condition; build a named anxiety-management plan. → *Can articulate their 2-3 technique plan.*

## Tristan de Montebello (Ultraspeaking)

19. **Fix root causes, not symptoms.** Filler words and racing come from discomfort with pausing under pressure: "Solve that, and the filler words take care of themselves." → *When the mind races, pauses silently instead of "um"/speeding up.*
20. **Think up, don't look down.** Looking down while thinking reads uncertain (3x worse on Zoom) and invites interruption. → *Thinking pauses go up/level.*
21. **End strong / land the plane.** Peak-end rule: a great answer that tapers into "...I don't know if that makes sense" is remembered by its landing. Summary prompts: "So to wrap up...", "What I want you to remember is..." → *Last sentence is a declarative close; no trailing hedge.*
22. **Stay in character, don't leak.** "People cannot see what you feel." Announcing your wobble ("sorry, that was rambly") is the pilot announcing an unexplained red light. Self-cue: "stay in it." → *Zero self-deprecating meta-commentary about the performance.*
23. **Speak conversationally.** Don't snap into announcer voice; "don't think about us, just think out loud" — discover your words with the audience. → *Pressured register matches normal chat register.*
24. **Enjoyment as barometer; hard reps only.** "The only reps worth something are the ones where you feel an edge." Kevin Kelly: "Pros are just amateurs who've learned to recover gracefully from their mistakes." → *Practices with deliberately hard constraints; keeps going through mistakes.*
25. **Conductor: energy leads, words follow.** Speaking at different energy levels unlocks different content; most people are comfortable in one band. Slowing down = willingness to take up space. → *Can shift volume/pace on cue without losing composure.*
26. **Triple Step: hold one direction through distraction.** Be "the tree in the storm" — make interruptions serve your thread. → *After a curveball, returns to and completes the original thread.*
27. **Choose a strong direction immediately.** Audible option-shopping kills answers: "the stronger the direction you choose in the beginning, the more ideas you're going to have." → *First sentence commits to one thesis.*
28. **Conviction prompts.** Injected starters ("I genuinely believe...", "This matters a ton because...") change the content that comes out; most people under-advocate. → *Uses first-person conviction statements, not uniform hedging.*
29. **The Accordion Method.** Prep by speaking, not writing: deliver aloud at 3 min → 2 → 1 → 30 sec, then expand back, re-adding only what serves. Result is internalized, plastic, mistake-proof. Carry only 4 lines: your one thing + three bookmarks. → *Can deliver a coherent 30-second version on demand; notes ≤4 cues.*
30. **Bow and Arrow.** "Stop focusing on what you want to say; focus on what you want them to remember." The arrow = the one sentence they'd remember. → *Can state the one thing in one sentence before delivering.*
31. **Bullshitter fear is a false alarm.** If the alarm fires, your detector will police you; "match that bullshitter's level of communication, except I'll have the ideas to back it up." → *Practices confident delivery of what they actually know.*
32. **Background listeners, not conscious monitoring.** Internalize habits until only a light ping remains ("you may be rambling — wrap it up"). → *Notices running long and wraps within one sentence smoothly.*

## Paige Costello (Asana/Intuit)

33. **Answer the question they should have asked.** Achievers answer one-to-one "like getting an A"; the exec-level move also addresses the bigger point behind the question. → *After the literal answer, surfaces the underlying concern.*
34. **Confidence through insight, not bravado.** "Know thy customer, market, competitors, numbers, product." Trust = (credibility + reliability + authenticity) / self-interest (Anne Raimondi). → *Claims are backed by a specific observation or number.*
35. **Confidence is conveyed by small braveries.** "Real confidence is being willing to say 'I don't know what you mean by that, can you say that again?'" "Say it before you're ready to say it." → *Asks a clarifying question or admits not-knowing instead of bluffing.*
36. **"How might the opposite be true?"** (Conscious Leadership, above/below the line). The reframe that drops the shoulders. → *Voices an alternative reading before acting on the catastrophized one.*
37. **Calibrated confidence on drafts.** "This is 70% finished. The 30% I don't believe is there yet are these three things." → *States confidence level and gaps unprompted.*
38. **Inquiry over advocacy; SBI feedback.** Situation-Behavior-Impact; triage received feedback with "must do, should do, or consider?" → *Feedback has time/place, behavior, felt impact.*

## Wes Kao

39. **Cut the preamble.** "Start right before you get eaten by the bear" — serve a sliver of context right before the juicy part. Companion: the super specific how. → *Reaches the point within two sentences; no wind-up.*
40. **Bottom line first.** Recommendation up top, "context:" below; separate decisions/asks from FYIs. Lenny: "just tell me what you think we should do, then help me understand why." → *Conclusion in the first sentence of an update.*
41. **State your recommendation; no fake-neutral framing.** A skewed pros/cons list masquerading as objective breeds suspicion; "my recommendation is X, here are the downsides" builds trust. → *Either states a labeled recommendation with downsides or asks a genuinely neutral question.*
42. **State changes hold attention.** Attention is 2-4 minutes; punctuate monologues with polls, questions, hand-offs. → *No unbroken monologue past ~4 minutes.*
43. **Eyes lighting up are data.** "If this person looks bored, they look bored — that is data." Trim what deadens, expand what lands. → *Adapts mid-conversation to energy.*
44. **Manage up with right-sized proactive context.** Scale context to decision reversibility; "you think you're over-communicating, but to the recipient it's just the right amount." → *Updates carry recommendation, reasoning, and the input needed.*
45. **Say no via trade-offs** (via Alex Peck). "Yes, and that means X waits until Friday — or do you want me to keep the original priority?" → *Names the displaced work and asks the requester to confirm the ordering.*
