# Composure & mindset

Sources: `podcasts/joe-hudson.md`, `podcasts/jerry-colonna.md`, `podcasts/carole-robin.md`, `podcasts/matt-mochary.md`, `podcasts/donna-lichaw.md`. Directly relevant to Ben's Self 1 / Self 2 frame from Inner Game of Tennis.

## Joe Hudson

1. **Respond to the critical voice, don't fight it.** The repeating inner critic is "always wrong" in framing even when right in content. Treat it like a scared child: "Oh, I see that you're really scared. Don't worry. I'm right here with you." Run daily experiments in responding, framed as learning, so failures can't trigger the quit response. → *Acknowledges the voice kindly instead of obeying or suppressing it.*
2. **The emotion you avoid is the one you invite.** "Whatever emotion you're trying to avoid, you are inviting into your life in exactly the way you're trying to avoid it." Backward-engineer any recurring problem to the feeling being dodged; be willing to feel it, then respond cleanly. → *Names and sits with the sting instead of defending or counterattacking.*
3. **It's where you come from, not what you say.** The scary CEO always has one person they don't yell at — the one who doesn't approach in fear. Work on your state before the conversation, not the script. → *Hard message delivered calm and warm, direct content, no fear or judgment in the framing.*
4. **Embrace intensity.** 3-5 standing principles make responses automatic. "If somebody says 'you're fucking up this business' — oh, cool, tell me what I'm missing." Or defer honestly: "Give me a day and I'll come back to you." → *Harsh criticism gets a curious follow-up or an explicit deferral, not a rebuttal.*
5. **Question the assumption to dissolve the problem.** "Is it a problem that I'm an asshole, or is the problem that I'm ashamed of being an asshole?" Shame holds the habit in place; unpack the hidden assumption in any limiting story. → *Unpacks at least one assumption behind a stated self-limit.*
6. **Enjoy it 10% more; experiments, not shoulds.** "If you figure out how to enjoy what you do 10% more, you are 10% more efficient." "Should" creates stagnation; wants create movement. → *Replaces "I should" with "I want to / I'll experiment with."*

## Jerry Colonna (Reboot)

7. **Radical self-inquiry.** "How have I been complicit in creating the conditions I say I don't want?" (Complicit — driving the getaway car, not robbing the bank.) Companions: What am I not saying that needs saying? What am I saying that's not being heard? → *Identifies their own contribution to a frustrating situation.*
8. **Detach self-esteem from outcomes.** The whispery voice ("they might find out") is temporarily quieted by busyness, fueling compulsive striving. "What if it doesn't matter to your sense of self-esteem if you succeed or fail?" → *Distinguishes the outcome's stakes from their own worth.*
9. **Sort the baggage before it charges interest.** Unexamined patterns leak into teams; the most powerful person has the most duty to self-examine. Parker Palmer: "If you choose to live an unexamined life, please don't take a job that involves other people." → *Names their own recurring pattern and its effect on others.*
10. **Truth-telling over crushing-it theater.** "Can we just not bullshit each other?" Peer circles that hold space without fixing. → *Includes at least one true difficulty in any "how's it going."*

## Carole Robin (Stanford "Touchy-Feely")

11. **The 15% rule.** Step 15% outside the comfort zone — enough to feel it, not enough to alarm anyone; disclosure is reciprocal and compounds. Appropriate vulnerability ("I have never needed you all more") vs. dumping ("I'm not sure I should be your VP"). → *Volunteers one slightly-uncomfortable honest disclosure.*
12. **Anger is secondary; lead with what's under it.** The CEO who said "I am deeply worried and afraid that I'm the only person here as concerned about this missed deadline as I am" — "I have never had my troops rally faster." → *Names the fear/hurt/worry underneath instead of the blame.*
13. **Stay on your side of the net.** You know your intent, the behavior, and the impact on you — not their motive. "I feel that you don't care" is an attribution, not a feeling. Formula: "When you do [behavior], I feel [feeling word], and I'm telling you because [investment]." → *No "you always / you don't care" attributions; behavior + own feeling.*
14. **Repair with "What did you hear me say?"** Nine times out of ten what they heard isn't what you said; don't defend intent, restate. "Talk about a pinch before it becomes a crunch." → *Checks what was heard before re-asserting.*
15. **Inquiry before advice; what/when/where/how, never why.** "Why" creates defensiveness; advice creates power differentials and usually misses. AFOG: "Another Fucking Opportunity for Growth"; first question after failure: "what did you learn?" → *Asks two open questions before offering any solution.*

## Matt Mochary

16. **Fear gives bad advice.** The gripped brain makes "very exaggerated predictions." His bet protocol: predict the outcome of the feared low-stakes action against a calm second — "I have made this bet hundreds of times and never lost." → *Names the fear-prediction out loud and tests it against a calmer read.*
17. **Tell the board the bad news.** "You think you're going to lose their trust. I think you're going to gain their trust." Disclosure + visible ownership + energy about solving = increased trust, every time. → *Surfaces bad news upward proactively, paired with ownership.*
18. **"I perceive you to be in anger."** The I-statement version punches through where "you're angry" (accusation) and "are you angry?" (passive-aggressive) fail. Anger covers pain; when in it, stop transacting — "you're breaking glass." → *Pauses when their own state is flagged.*
19. **Warn the amygdala.** "This is going to be a difficult conversation. Take a few seconds and prepare yourself." Then plain delivery, then invite the feelings: "My guess is you're feeling a lot of anger, fear, sadness. Is that true?" → *Gives the heads-up before hard news; invites the reaction after.*
20. **Make them feel heard by voicing the unspoken.** Reflect the thoughts behind the words, deliberately stronger: "I think you're thinking, screw you, Matt, how dare you..." — then close the loop with what you'll do or why not. → *Paraphrases including the emotional charge; gets a "yes, that's it" before responding.*

## Donna Lichaw

21. **Audit self-stories with real data.** "They may or may not be true; our brain doesn't know the difference." Treat "nobody listens to me" as a hypothesis; go collect data. When true, the fix is often style communication: "I'm a slow processor; I'm listening." → *States the self-belief as testable and proposes the check.*
22. **Ask how the impostor feeling serves you.** "Okay, that's a good story. How is it serving you?" Often it's a growth-edge alarm that triggers preparation; find the dose. "You're probably the first person to ever have your role — embrace it." → *Identifies what the feeling prompts and where it overshoots.*
23. **Superpowers from peak stories; head-heart-hands.** Mine three peak stories for recurring strengths; scale exposure through tiny experiments; evaluate through thoughts, emotion, and body ("'fine' said with a bright-red face is data"). → *Reports the body signal too; commits to a small next experiment.*
24. **"Isn't that interesting?"** Under provocation (talked over 20 times), narrate the raw experience neutrally before choosing a response. → *Neutral observation precedes the reaction.*

## Convergences

Anger covers pain (Robin #12, Mochary #18). Self-story as hypothesis (Hudson #5, Colonna #7, Lichaw #21). Impostor handling: detach worth from outcome (Colonna #8) + ask how it serves you (Lichaw #22) — complementary, not identical.
