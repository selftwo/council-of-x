# Career conversations: negotiation, growth, interviews

Sources: `newsletters/the-10-commandments-of-salary-negotiation.md` (Niya Dragova), `newsletters/the-ultimate-guide-to-negotiating-your-comp.md` (Jacob Warwick), `newsletters/the-magic-loop.md` + `podcasts/ethan-evans-20.md`, `newsletters/how-to-get-promoted.md`, `newsletters/how-to-pass-any-first-round-interview-even-in-a-terrible-talent-market.md` (Coach Erika), `podcasts/phyl-terry.md`.

## Salary/offer negotiation

1. **Never give a number first; ask for the band** (Dragova). "Can you tell me the salary band for this level? Happy to let you know if it's within my range; we can discuss specifics when I've met the team." Recruiter's "give me your number, I'll make it happen" = "I'll get you something lower but close." → *Deflects the expectations question without naming a number.*
2. **Reopen with new information, not aggression** (Dragova). Other conversations and expected ranges suffice (never show offer letters — cite NDA; never quote Glassdoor; never threaten to walk; phone, not email — "negotiating via email = MAJOR CRINGE"). "Instead of asking for money, make them give you more by bringing in obstacles the recruiter needs to overcome." → *Introduces obstacles (other talks, retention pressure, expanded scope), doesn't re-argue qualifications.*
3. **Total comp first, components later, signing bonus last** (Dragova). Read the offer as data: low = weak advocate; template = wait for a manager batting for you; top-of-band = push for uplevel. → *Negotiates the overall number before components.*
4. **GAINS** (Warwick): Gather intelligence, Align as a peer exploring solutions, Influence via champions, Navigate with patience, Secure with precise agreements. Weak: "I have 15 years of experience..." Strong: "Your PMF challenges remind me of a situation at X... how aligned are your product and research teams on an ICP?" → *Connects experience to their specific problem and ends with a question.*
5. **Enthusiasm plus space** (Warwick). "I'm genuinely excited and can see myself making an impact. I'd love to discuss a few details to make sure we're aligned." "What's the chance we could explore a slightly different structure?" Never accept in under 3-5 business days. → *Every counter pairs enthusiasm with a question-shaped ask.*
6. **Transparent timelines** (Warwick). "I have a few conversations in progress, but I'm most excited about this. I expect to decide by [date]. Does that work with your process?" → *States a self-set decision date; mentions alternatives without weaponizing.*
7. **Define every vague term** (Warwick). "When you say [significant equity / successful integration], what specifically would success look like in six months? What metrics?" ($20M lost undocumented; $1.2M gained defined.) → *Flags each vague term and asks for a number or definition.*
8. **Sell the vacation** (Warwick). Describe the org 30/90/180 days after you join, tied to their metrics ("By month three, we'd see a 30% reduction in scope creep"). Bold question that won an uplevel: "How much of the team's failure to grow do you take ownership of... and how can I best show up for you?" — then hold the silence. → *Concrete first-quarter plan with numbers; silence after the bold question.*
9. **Value before day one** (Warwick). Follow-ups deliver an insight or artifact, not thanks; a prototype earned one candidate +30%. → *Follow-ups contain something new for them.*

## Growth and promotion

10. **The Magic Loop** (Ethan Evans): do your job well (manager-verified), ask how you can help your manager, do it, then ask to help in a way that also advances a named goal, repeat. Scripts: "It's been a while since I checked in... could you tell me how you feel I am doing?" (say thank you, don't argue). "One day I'd like to be promoted. Could I help out in a way that you think would develop me toward that goal?" → *Frames the growth ask as mutual and names the goal explicitly.*
11. **Advanced Loop: suggest, then just do it** (Evans). "I noticed we're often late because we forget vacation schedules... I could add a step that captures these. Would that be helpful, or is there something else you'd like me to do?" At capacity: name a specific low-value task to hand off ("discard a 3 and add a jack"). → *Proposals end with the choice question; overload states the specific trade.*
12. **The gap conversation** (Lenny). "People don't get promoted for doing their jobs really well. They get promoted by demonstrating their potential to do more" (Tara Jaye Frank). Push your manager to name the 2-3 gap skills, build an action plan, monthly review; tell them plainly the promotion matters to you; keep a wins doc for your champion's ammo. → *Elicits a finite named gap list with a review cadence.*
13. **Crisis recovery** (Evans). Own it in sentence one; proactive hourly updates with a stated next-update time ("buying life one hour at a time"); then face the angry leader in person — "angry emails are easy." → *Ownership first sentence + committed next update time.*

## Interviews and story

14. **One decisive reason** (Coach Erika). No hygiene reasons (comp, commute) or complaint-shaped reasons. "I've spent eight years building systems for 100M users. An exciting step would be building for the billion-user scale." → *One forward-looking reason that names what they bring.*
15. **JD mirroring** (Erika). Two-column table of the posting's keywords to your experiences; answer in their vocabulary. → *Reuses the posting's exact words.*
16. **STAR++ and the three formats** (Erika). Pure behavioral (STAR + what I learned + how I applied it later); theoretical (outline elements, end with an example); situational (clarify, state assumptions, plan, check in: "Did I miss any key steps from your perspective?"). Prep 3-5 deep recent projects, not scripted answers. → *Answers end with the learning and a later application; hypotheticals open with clarifying questions.*
17. **Win it with your questions** (Erika). Questions that show researched homework and can't be answered from the posting. → *End-of-interview questions cite specific researched facts.*
18. **Impact over effort; enthusiasm** (Evans). "Are you bringing me an understanding of the business, or just telling me how hard you worked?" "I might've decided I don't want the job. I still want the offer." → *Every accomplishment includes the business impact; explicit enthusiasm.*

## Job search (Phyl Terry, Never Search Alone)

19. **Candidate-market fit.** Mnookin two-pager (wants/don't-wants) → listening tour (reverse exit interviews: "What did I do well? What am I a fit for? Am I projecting myself accurately?"; the golden question: "If you were in my shoes, how would you approach this?") → a 3-4 attribute one-sentence CMF — "you need a spear and not a net." Close the loop with monthly updates even with no news. → *Asks the golden question instead of "got a job for me?"; target stated in 3-4 concrete attributes.*
20. **Job Mission with OKRs → four-legs negotiation.** Draft the job's mission/OKRs yourself; share live: "I've thought about what the role is. Can I share something with you?" (Amazon leader: "I would hire them on the spot.") On offer: success conditions before money ("Before money — I think there's $10M of tech debt here. Are we on board that's priority one?"; balking is "a huge red flag"), then the low-risk money ask: "Are you open to 450? That's what I was hoping for — is that something we can talk about?" (asking works 87% of the time). Negotiate with the hiring manager, by phone. Debrief every interview with a council — your inner critic misreads how it went. → *Ties asks to agreed success outcomes before naming comp; comp ask phrased "are you open to X?"*

## Cross-cutting (Warwick's applicant-mode warning signs — transcript-checkable)

Talking >60% of the time; past-focused examples with no bridge to their current challenges; staying formal instead of collaborative problem-solving.
