## The 15 distilled coachable behaviors (eval seeds)

Observable in a practice transcript; each cites its anchor. These are what the judge and the coach whisper should check for.

1. **Headline first**: states the point/recommendation in the first two sentences, before context. (Kao bottom-line-first; Abrahams PREP)
2. **No wind-up**: reaches the substance within two sentences; no biographical preamble. (Kao "start right before the bear")
3. **One direction, committed**: first sentence of a spontaneous answer commits to one thesis; no audible option-shopping. (de Montebello)
4. **Structured, not listed**: answers have a recognizable shape (PREP or What/So What/Now What), not a stream. (Abrahams)
5. **Lands the plane**: last sentence is a declarative close; no trailing "...if that makes sense," "...I don't know." (de Montebello, peak-end)
6. **No leaking**: zero self-deprecating meta-commentary ("sorry, that was rambly") and no preemptive apologies ("this might be a dumb idea, but"). (de Montebello; Pfeffer)
7. **Pauses instead of fillers**: when the mind races, goes silent rather than "um"/accelerating. (de Montebello root-cause)
8. **Conviction stated**: uses first-person conviction ("I believe X because") rather than uniform hedging; a labeled recommendation with honest downsides. (de Montebello; Kao)
9. **Paraphrase before pushback**: plays the other person's concern back (with its emotional charge) and gets a "yes" before countering. (Walter; Mochary)
10. **Disagrees with a question**: challenges logic via "help me see how X connects to Y," not "I disagree." (LaPointe)
11. **Their goals in the ask**: frames requests in the listener's stated goals/metrics; asks "what evidence would convince you?" (Walter; Lenny)
12. **Names the trade**: pushback names the specific thing that slips and its company-level cost; new asks get a priority-confirmation question. (Seshan; Kao/Peck)
13. **Explicit ask**: says what they want out loud, paired with acknowledgment of the other's right to decide. (Berger)
14. **Own side of the net**: complaints are behavior + own feeling; no motive attributions ("you don't care"). (Robin)
15. **Composure moves visible**: under provocation, a neutral observation or clarifying question precedes the response; can say "I don't know what you mean, say that again." (Lichaw; Costello)

Anti-behaviors the coach should flag by name: rambling past the point (2), option-shopping aloud (3), leaking (6), filling silence (7), hedged fake-neutral framing (8), motive attribution (14), applicant mode — talking >60% of the time and past-focused answers with no bridge to the listener's problem (Warwick).
