# Influence & persuasion without authority

Sources: `newsletters/a-pms-guide-to-influence.md` (Jules Walter), `podcasts/kenneth-berger.md`, `podcasts/jeffrey-pfeffer.md`, `podcasts/evan-lapointe.md`, `newsletters/how-to-get-better-at-influence.md`, `newsletters/this-week-9-breaking-into-growth-leading-with-influence-and-not-stepping-on-toes.md`.

## Jules Walter (Slack/YouTube)

1. **Seek intel on how each stakeholder decides.** Before pitching, run 1:1s to learn their goals, competing projects, who they listen to, and likely concerns; signal you're gathering, not selling: "This is meant to be an informal chat. I'd like to better understand your world and get your advice." → *Asks discovery questions about their goals and concerns before any pitch.*
2. **Frame from their POV.** Stakeholders are focused on their own problems. His Butterfield example: monetization repitched as user benefit. Chris Voss: "Negotiation is the art of letting the other side have your way." → *States the ask in the listener's goals/metrics, not the asker's.*
3. **Pre-wire the meeting before the meeting.** Meet detractors to understand objections ("Can you walk me through your concerns? Are you most concerned about A or B?"); brief champions and ask for specific in-meeting support ("Any chance you could chime in or pre-brief X?"). 14+ pre-meetings before his YouTube exec summit; strategy voted top 3. → *Names detractors/champions and arranges alignment before the decision moment.*
4. **Make people feel heard before you push.** Feeling dismissed triggers fight/flight and louder repetition. Play back their concerns; synthesize periodically for progressive alignment. Influence is reciprocal. → *Paraphrases the concern back before responding to it.*
5. **Conduct the meeting.** Open with the desired decision ("Our goal today is approval on the go-to-market plan"), park tangents with time references, adapt format to the exec (silent-read memos, skip context slides). → *Opens with an explicit decision goal; redirects with time references.*

## Kenneth Berger (ex-Slack, exec coach)

6. **Articulate, ask, accept.** People-pleasers hint; control freaks order; both fail. His Slack story: a year of 1:1s never saying what he wanted; one explicit written ask got his job back in a week. "When you actually ask for what you want out loud, you're much more likely to get it." → *Makes an explicit out-loud ask, not hints or complaints.*
7. **Clear ask + humility.** "I really disagree with this decision and I'd prefer we make this different one. I know it's not my call and I'm just one opinion, but it's important to me that you know that. Are you willing to reconsider?" Conviction is legitimate currency even without data. → *Direct preference + acknowledgment of their right to decide + a question.*
8. **Only a hell yes is a yes.** "Maybe we can deliver by May 1" predictably slips. "What would be a hell yes date for you?" Respecting a no at face value often motivates more than coercion. → *Names tepid agreement as a no and asks what would make it enthusiastic.*
9. **Find the dream behind the complaint.** "Every complaint implies a dream." Convert the frustration into a designed ask likely to get a yes. → *Converts venting into a forward-looking request.*
10. **Feedback: behavior, feeling, request** (via Carole Robin). "When you do X, I feel Y, and I'm telling you because Z." "I feel like you're an asshole" is a story, not a feeling. → *Observable behavior + real feeling word + explicit request.*

## Jeffrey Pfeffer (Stanford, Power)

11. **No preemptive apologies.** Never open with "I don't know if this is useful..." — "if it's not useful don't say it, and never apologize for speaking." Disempowering self-descriptions leak. "If you want to be liked, get a dog." → *Zero apologetic hedges before statements.*
12. **Just ask.** People overestimate refusal (Frank Flynn's research). "If they say no, you are no worse off than had you not asked. Get over your ego, ask." → *Makes the ask despite expecting a no.*
13. **Show up powerfully.** People respond to how you look, then sound, least to content. Behaviors: no notes, eye contact, brevity, no apologizing, gestures, open posture, louder voice, humor. "If you can get people to laugh, you can tell them anything" (Rushdie). These are learnable. → *Brief, declarative, unscripted, uses humor.*
14. **Suspend judgment of people on your critical path.** The only relevant fact is you need their cooperation; leaked disrespect drives cooperation "essentially to zero." Reduce friction rather than overpower it. → *No contemptuous language about needed counterparts; engages their needs.*
15. **Brand, generosity, brokerage.** "You cannot choose what is not in your head" — visibility plus substance. Networking's first principle is generosity; become a broker of useful introductions; weak ties carry the best opportunities (Granovetter). → *Offers value alongside the request.*

## Evan LaPointe

16. **Prime before deciding.** State the category of conversation, the mindset, the principles — under three minutes or in the invite. Speed-optimizer vs. accuracy-optimizer without priming = "a cat fight." Debate principles, not tactics. → *Opens by stating purpose, decision type, and principles.*
17. **Activate the prefrontal cortex, not the amygdala.** "I completely disagree" initiates combat; every brain shifts to winning. Instead: "I have a question," "Can we pull that thread? You're connecting these two dots, I'm not seeing how they're connected." → *Disagreement voiced as a logic question, not a verdict.*
18. **Choose your character and speed.** Get permission for your role up front ("I'm the devil's advocate guy — okay if I do that?"). Moderate speed = Challenger move: teach a new signal, "let them know it and let them live with it" — in 30 days "everywhere I look, I can't not see it." → *Gives a signal to watch for and defers the close.*
19. **Relationships gate influence: ability x trust x appeal — appeal dominates.** A brilliant miserable-to-work-with person gets sequestered from information flow. Diagnostic: "what kind of experience am I?" Disclose your own thresholds vulnerably ("this is inconceivable to me because of how my brain works — give me something concrete"). → *Brings positive energy; discloses own bias and asks for a concrete translation instead of dismissing.*

## Lenny Rachitsky

20. **Make their goals your goals; charge the trust battery; slow is fast.** 10 seconds on what they want before asking (Superhost badge repitched as guest conversion + A/B test); Tobi Lütke's trust battery; loop people in early, invite objections. Evidence priority: hard data > synthesized research > anecdotes > competitive analysis > analogues; ask "What evidence would convince you this is the right decision?" → *Names their goal inside the ask; offers a test; asks what evidence would change their mind.*
21. **Getting unstuck in bureaucracy.** Find the actual decision maker ("Who's the decision-maker on this?"); ask "What would it take to convince you this is worth prioritizing?"; get pre-buy-in from whoever would do the work; set a date ("I'd like to make a call by X to keep things moving"); for ambiguous ownership: communicate, then proceed. → *Names the decision maker, asks the convince-you question, proposes a decision date.*
