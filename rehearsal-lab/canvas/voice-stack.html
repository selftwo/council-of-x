Pipecat is the open-source Python framework that runs this loop in real time: it listens while you talk, detects when your turn actually ends (a small local model judges whether the sentence is finished, not just whether you paused — important for how you speak), lets you interrupt the persona mid-reply, and logs every turn with timing. It ships a DeepSeek service class, so the calibrated brain drops in as-is.

the eval loop survives voice untouched

what stays exactly the same

runs/<id>/ folders, prompt hash versioning, transcript.jsonl schema, checks.mjs, both judges, report.html, this canvas. A voice run is just another run folder. Node stays the source of truth.

what gets added

one thin Python file (voice/bot.py) that runs the Pipecat pipeline and writes our transcript format, plus new per-turn fields only voice can have: was the bot interrupted, gap between you stopping and it speaking, per-service latency, audio recordings of both sides.

the one integration risk to verify at build time: Pipecat's DeepSeek service doesn't document the thinking-disabled flag our harness needs (v4 flash reasoning drains max_tokens without it). if it can't pass through, we subclass the service — small, known work.

Read the bars this way: even daily practice costs a coffee per month. DeepSeek is a rounding error ($0.0003, measured from our own ledger, ~90% cache hits). STT covers all 2 minutes but is cheap per minute; TTS speaks only ~1,000 characters but is the priciest line. So the stack choice is about how the voice feels, not what it costs. Free credits ($200 Deepgram, $50 AssemblyAI) make the STT side effectively free for the whole calibration era.

speech-to-speech: checked, ruled out

The vault flagged OpenAI Realtime (one model listens, thinks, and speaks) as the alternative to compare before committing to Pipecat. Verdict from both independent workers: ✗ ruled out. The decisive reason: the brain must stay DeepSeek, because the persona prompts are calibrated on it; in Realtime, OpenAI's own model runs the conversation and DeepSeek could only be a tool it consults. It also happens to be worse on voice quality (ranks below dedicated TTS), slower to first audio (0.8–2.3s vs 0.1–0.25s), and 7–15× the cost. Revisit only if the DeepSeek requirement ever drops.

how you decide (codex's method, kept): a fixed ~20-line audition script of pushback lines — interruption, skepticism, corrective feedback, calm authority, one sharp challenge — rendered by all three, listened to on your phone and headphones. Voice choice is more voice-specific than vendor-specific; your ear is the eval. The harness swaps the winner in with one line.
