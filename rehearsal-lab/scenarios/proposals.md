# Scenario proposals — pick the ones that feel like your life

15 candidates, grouped by what they train. Each is a difficult or interesting conversation tuned to Ben: put-on-the-spot pressure, AI/product/career topics, workplace dynamics. Mark keep/kill/modify; kept ones become real scenario files with corpus-grounded personas.

**Ben's picks (2026-07-18): A1, A3, B6, C8, C9, D11, D12, D13, E14, E15 — 10 scenarios approved, marked ✅ below. Build queue in that order.**

## A. Put on the spot (trains: composure, headline-first answers, not rambling)

1. ✅ **The skip-level ambush.** A senior leader catches you after a meeting: "I keep hearing your name — what is it you're actually driving this quarter, and why does it matter?" You get 90 seconds before they're pulled away. The persona times you, interrupts rambles, rewards a crisp headline.
2. **"Why not just use ChatGPT?"** You're demoing an AI feature you built. An exec interrupts mid-demo with the dismissive classic. Practice absorbing the jab, not getting defensive, and reframing to the actual problem it solves.
3. ✅ **The cold call.** In a leadership meeting you're mostly observing, the room turns: "You've been quiet — what's your take?" The topic is adjacent to your expertise, not core. Practice buying two seconds, taking a position, and scoping honestly what you don't know.

## B. Founder and technology debates (trains: conviction quality, debating without anxiety)

4. **"AI kills the PM role."** A founder friend, smart and provocative, argues product management is dead. They genuinely push. Practice disagreeing with someone you like without wilting or steamrolling.
5. **The skeptic CTO.** You propose building a feature on LLMs; a sharp CTO wants deterministic software: "non-deterministic products are demos, not products." You've literally built these things — practice bringing that lived evidence to bear under pushback.
6. ✅ **Hot-take sparring.** A big industry event just happened (model release, acquisition, pricing change). A sharp operator wants your read and attacks weak reasoning. Rotating current-events debate partner.

## C. Workplace difficulty (trains: managing up, holding ground, boundaries)

7. **Stakeholder pushback (Rajan).** Already built — the skeptical enterprise VP. Keep as-is?
8. ✅ **The steamroller peer.** A confident peer talks over you, rebrands your idea as theirs in front of others, and is "just being efficient." Practice interrupting politely, reclaiming the point, and not letting it slide — in the moment, not in a follow-up doc.
9. ✅ **The slipping date.** You have to tell your manager a committed date will slip. Practice delivering bad news headline-first with a plan, instead of context-first apologetic rambling.
10. **No to a pet feature.** Someone senior "just has a quick idea" they want squeezed in. Practice a warm, firm no that survives the second and third ask.

## D. Career conversations (trains: telling your story, negotiating)

11. ✅ **The promo case.** Performance review; you must narrate your own impact without cringing or underclaiming. The persona probes: "that sounds like the team's work — what was *yours*?"
12. ✅ **The interview gauntlet.** "Walk me through a product you built" with hostile follow-ups on metrics, failures, and "why did that take so long?" Also covers telling the solo-AI-builder story to someone who doesn't yet believe it.
13. ✅ **The negotiation.** Offer stage or scope/comp conversation. The persona anchors low, uses silence, and tests whether you can hold a number without filling the air.

## E. Expression drills (trains: structure, clarity — coach-led rather than adversarial)

14. ✅ **The 60-second drill.** A coach fires unpredictable spot questions (product, AI, career, opinion). Every answer must lead with the headline. Coach scores structure immediately after each answer, then fires the next. Reps, not role-play.
15. ✅ **Explain it to a sharp outsider.** Explain something you know deeply (RAG, evals, why non-deterministic products are hard) to a smart non-tech person who keeps probing the fuzzy parts. Practice de-jargoning without dumbing down.
