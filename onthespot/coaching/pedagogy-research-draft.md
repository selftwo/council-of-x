## 1. What real speaking coaching measures, and in what order

The four traditions we studied measure different things, and the differences matter for what onthespot should automate.

**Matt Abrahams (Stanford GSB).** His book is ordered as six steps: Calm, Unlock, Redefine, Listen, Structure, Focus. The first four are mindset and attention; only the last two touch the message. So the explicit ordering is anxiety first, technique second, because anxiety consumes the cognitive resources spontaneous speaking needs ([GSB masterclass](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/how-think-faster-talk-smarter-masterclass-matt-abrahams), [book summary](http://wisewords.blog/miscellaneous/think-faster-talk-smarter-book-summary-matt-abrahams/)). His assessment is qualitative: diagnose your anxiety type (Affective, Behavioral, Cognitive), build a 3 to 5 technique Anxiety Management Plan, and evaluate messages on audience outcomes (what should they Know, Feel, Do) and four message criteria (precision, relevance, accessibility, concision). He offloads numeric delivery metrics to apps (Poised, Orai, LikeSo). He has no numeric progression system (inference from the sources above). Practice doctrine: "prepare to be spontaneous"; repetition, reflection, and outside feedback, because "we are not the best judges of our communication" (`../../sources/lenny-knowledge-graph-vault/podcasts/matt-abrahams.md`, ~41:24).

**Ultraspeaking (de Montebello and Gendler).** Games isolate one sub-skill each and create deliberate "turbulence" under low stakes: Conductor (energy range), Triple Step (holding one direction through distraction), Rapid Fire Analogies (trusting auto-complete), Conviction Prompts (committed first-person claims) ([Lenny episode](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-public-speaking-tristan-de-montebello), [Triple Step page](https://ultraspeaking.com/triple-step-game/), transcript at `../../sources/lenny-knowledge-graph-vault/podcasts/tristan-de-montebello.md`). Their measurement unit is the felt experience of each rep plus a one-or-two-variable change for the next rep: after every rep the coach asks "What did you like about that? How did that feel?" before anything else ([Marie Poulin review](https://mariepoulin.com/blog/honest-review-ultraspeaking/)). They explicitly refuse to count symptoms: "instead of counting my filler words... figure out the root cause... Solve that, and the filler words take care of themselves" (de Montebello transcript, ~13:24). The habits they do treat as trainable one at a time: end strong, stay in character (no leaking), think up, choose a strong direction, speak conversationally.

**Toastmasters.** The opposite pole: measurement is institutionalized into narrow single-metric observer roles. The Ah-Counter logs filler sounds and crutch words per speaker every meeting ([official role](https://www.toastmasters.org/membership/club-meeting-roles/ah-counter)); the Timer reports elapsed time per speaker; the Grammarian tracks language use ([roles index](https://www.toastmasters.org/membership/club-meeting-roles)). Evaluations follow Commend, Recommend, Commend and cap recommendations at one or two things the speaker can actually change ([Toastmaster magazine on the sandwich](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2018/nov2018/29-toastmasters-toolbox), [Effective Evaluation manual PDF](https://ccdn.toastmasters.org/medias/files/department-documents/education-documents/202-effective-evaluation.pdf)). Pathways sequences five levels from fundamentals to integrated performance, and Level 1 ends with a give-a-speech, get-feedback, apply-it-in-the-next-speech loop ([Pathways overview](https://www.toastmasters.org/pathways-overview/pathways-dynamic-leadership-path)).

**Executive communication coaching.** Supplies the norm values. Filler words: about 1 per minute is optimal, the average speaker uses 5 per minute (Noah Zandan, HBR, from 4,000+ analyzed samples, [PDF](https://foodsci.rutgers.edu/Graduate/PDF/GettingPastUmm.pdf)). Speaking rate: 100 to 165 wpm is the range audiences process best ([Microsoft Speaker Coach](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/suggestions-from-speaker-coach-25e7d866-c895-4aa1-9b90-089b70a4ea38)). Great speakers pause 2 to 3 seconds; the average speaker pauses too little (Zandan, same source). Answer ordering: conclusion first (Minto Pyramid Principle, [explainer](https://www.betterup.com/blog/minto-pyramid)), which is the same claim as Kao's bottom-line-first in the corpus (`../../rehearsal-lab/coaching/01-speaking-under-pressure.md`, items 39-40). Engagements run 6 to 12 months with sessions every 2 to 4 weeks, so roughly 12 sessions, with re-measurement around 8 to 12 weeks ([engagement length](https://www.belindablock.com/faq/how-long-does-executive-coaching-last/), [measuring impact](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/how-to-measure-the-impact-of-executive-coaching-set-defined-goals/)). Communication-specific standardized assessments are rare; most coaches use general 360s, which is a gap a rubric-driven product can fill (research agent finding; moderate confidence).

**Deliberate practice (Ericsson).** The practice-design rules everyone above converges on: well-defined specific goals, one aspect of performance at a time, full concentration, immediate informative feedback, tasks just beyond current ability, designed by a coach (Ericsson, Krampe, Tesch-Romer 1993, [Psychological Review paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224827585_The_Role_of_Deliberate_Practice_in_the_Acquisition_of_Expert_Performance); Ericsson and Pool, Peak, 2016, [summary](https://commoncog.com/peak-book-summary/)). Plateaus happen when a skill goes automatic and stops responding to feedback; the fix is pulling one component back into conscious control ([Ericsson and Towne 2010 PDF](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/Expertise-Wiley-Interdisciplinary-Reviews-Cognitive-Science-Vol.-1-Issue-3-%E2%80%93-2010-BZID-2004.pdf)). Honesty note: the effect size of deliberate practice on expertise is contested ([Macnamara et al. 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160289613000421), [2019 replication](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/6/8/190327/68523/The-role-of-deliberate-practice-in-expert)). The design conditions remain good pedagogy; the "practice explains most of expertise" claim does not hold up.

**The synthesized measurement order** (this ordering is our synthesis, not any single source):

1. Anxiety and mindset state (Abrahams first four steps; Ultraspeaking makes this implicit through game design). Mostly self-reported in V1.
2. Endings and leaks: land the plane, no self-sabotaging meta-commentary. Cheapest observable fixes; single-habit, high-visibility (de Montebello peak-end argument, transcript ~18:53).
3. Lead position and commitment: point first, one direction (Kao, Abrahams PREP, de Montebello strong direction).
4. Structure shape of long turns (Abrahams structures).
5. Interaction moves: answer the asked question, paraphrase before pushback, explicit ask (corpus sections 02, 03).
6. Delivery mechanics: fillers, rate, pause profile (Toastmasters counts, Zandan norms). Partly V1 via local audio, fully V2.
7. Energy range, tone, prosody (Conductor). V2.
8. Gaze, gesture, body language (think up, Pfeffer's show-up-powerfully behaviors). V3.

## 2. How baselines are set in real coaching

- **Ultraspeaking:** a raw unprepped rep. "Just speak for 60 seconds so we get a baseline" is literally their intake move (de Montebello transcript, opening minute and ~10:37 of part 2). The baseline is the untouched behavior under mild pressure, recorded before any instruction.
- **Toastmasters:** the Ice Breaker speech plus the standing counts (Ah-Counter, Timer) that every meeting produces, so a member's numbers accumulate passively from day one ([Pathways Level 1](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2021/nov/improved-pathways-level-1)).
- **Executive coaching:** goals set up front, then 360 or stakeholder interviews as the reference point, re-run at 60 and 120 days ([source](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/how-to-measure-the-impact-of-executive-coaching-set-defined-goals/)).
- **Ericsson:** current performance is the reference; the coach designs drills at the gap edge.

For onthespot this translates to: session 1 of any journey is played with no coaching pressure, scored silently, and stored as the baseline record (schema in the rubric draft, section 3). The soft timer runs so time use is part of the baseline. Ben's post-session self-report ("what felt hardest") is captured because both Ultraspeaking (felt experience per rep) and Abrahams (self-diagnosed anxiety type) treat the subjective read as primary data, and because judge calibration needs his labels anyway.

## 3. A 10-session arc (proposed)

Grounded in: one fix at a time (Ericsson; Toastmasters 1-2 recommendations; Ultraspeaking one-variable changes between reps), difficulty just beyond comfort (Ericsson; de Montebello "the only reps worth something are the ones where you feel an edge", `../../rehearsal-lab/coaching/01-speaking-under-pressure.md` item 24), and re-measurement at the end (exec coaching cadence). The specific session assignments are proposal, not sourced doctrine.

- **Session 1, baseline.** One scenario from the family, target duration, no coaching. Full rubric run, silently. Output: baseline record plus the ranked failure list.
- **Session 2 to 3, the smallest fix.** Pick the single cheapest visible failure. Default candidates in order: un-said endings (P3), leaks (P4), then buried lead (P1). Rationale: endings and leaks are single-habit fixes with immediate cosmetic payoff, which is why Ultraspeaking teaches end strong and stay in character before anything content-related, and the reinforcing loop ("people react to you as confident, so you feel confident", transcript ~25:21) makes them self-sustaining. The debrief names one fix and one strength (CRC shape, minus the score theater).
- **Session 4 to 6, lead and commitment.** Point in the first two sentences; one direction held. Scenario variations ratchet pressure: a more aggressive persona move set, a shorter target time, an interruption mid-answer (Triple Step logic: distraction that must serve the thread). The previous fix is tracked but not coached unless it regresses.
- **Session 7 to 8, structure and interaction.** Recognizable shape on long turns (P6); answering the asked question (P5); the scenario's specific move from the corpus (paraphrase before pushback, the explicit ask, naming the trade).
- **Session 9, hard variant.** Same scenario family at maximum pressure: hostile framing, compressed time (an Accordion-style compression: deliver the same position in half the target time, de Montebello transcript ~703 region). Everything measured, one fix coached.
- **Session 10, re-baseline.** The session 1 variant repeated as exactly as possible. Delta report over the full metric vector and per-mode pass rates. This is the number the journey promised: measurably more coherent.

Expectations honesty: exec coaching literature says visible behavior change takes 8 to 12 weeks; a 10-session arc compressed into fewer weeks may show metric movement (endings, lead position) before habit movement (hedging under pressure). The corpus supports quick wins on the cosmetic habits and slower movement on root causes; do not promise root-cause change by session 10.

## 5. What only works with voice or video (do not fake in V1)

- **Filler density and live pause behavior.** V1's audio is Wispr Flow dictation: Ben can pause freely without anyone holding the floor against him, and can implicitly re-take a sentence. Filler and pause numbers from that audio are real signals about his thinking rhythm but are NOT equivalent to holding silence in a live conversation. Record them, trend them, label them "dictation-context" in reports, and do not claim behavior 7 is trained until V2. (Inference from the product design; check against a few real sessions.)
- **Prosodic tapering and end-strong delivery.** V1 can catch un-saying words (C4); it cannot catch the trailing-off voice. V2.
- **Energy range, monotony, tone (Conductor territory).** Entirely V2. Text has no register bandwidth worth judging.
- **Interruptions and floor dynamics.** Text turns cannot be interrupted mid-sentence, so Triple Step-style pressure and behavior 15's real-time composure are V2.
- **Think up, gaze, gesture, posture** (de Montebello think-up; Pfeffer show-up-powerfully). V3.
- **Nervous laughter and vocal leaking.** P4 in V1 only sees typed-out leaks; the vocal versions are V2.

V1's honest claim: it measures thinking, structure, and word choice under conversational pressure with time visibility, plus dictation-context pause data. That is most of behaviors 1 to 6, 8, and the scenario moves 9 to 14.

## 6. Where the outside methodology contradicts the corpus or the product design

1. **Counting versus root causes.** Ultraspeaking explicitly rejects counting fillers as coaching; Toastmasters institutionalizes counting them. The corpus took de Montebello's side (behavior 7 targets the pause, not the count). Resolution for onthespot: count everything in the harness (counts are free and make deltas computable), but never make a count the coached fix; the coached fix is the root-cause behavior, and the count is how we verify it moved. The rubric draft is built this way on purpose.
2. **Judgment-free reps versus rubric grading.** Ultraspeaking coaches lead with "what did you like about that?" and avoid critique-first feedback; onthespot runs two rubrics on every session. Resolution: keep grading out of the session and in the post-session report, keep the persona from grading Ben mid-session (persona rubric FM1 already enforces this), and cap the debrief at one fix plus one strength (Toastmasters CRC logic). The full metric table exists for the machinery, not for the debrief.
3. **Self-watching.** de Montebello is "not very much a fan of actually watching yourself on camera... the fundamentals are inside" (transcript, ~49:59). V3 as currently framed (body language feedback from a vision model) leans the other way. Not a V1 problem, but note it before building V3: the strongest version may be model-observed feedback without making Ben re-watch himself.
4. **Anxiety first versus message first.** Abrahams orders mindset before technique; the practicer rubric's six modes are all message-level. Partial mitigation: text chat plus dictation is a lower-anxiety medium, which is exactly why V1 starts there. But the arc should still capture a self-reported state line per session (the rubric's baseline record includes one), and the composure corpus (`../../rehearsal-lab/coaching/06-composure-mindset.md`) becomes central in V2 when real-time pressure arrives.
5. **Enjoyment versus effort.** Ericsson: deliberate practice is effortful and "not inherently enjoyable." Ultraspeaking: "if it doesn't feel enjoyable, you're doing it wrong," because unenjoyable practice gets abandoned. These genuinely disagree in the literature. For a single-user product whose failure mode is Ben not using it daily, side with Ultraspeaking on session design (games, short reps, visible wins) and with Ericsson on session content (edge-of-ability, one goal, immediate feedback).
6. **Hedging is not uniformly bad.** The powerless-language literature says hedges reduce perceived credibility ([Hosman and Siltanen 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0261927X11407169?download=true)), but newer work finds personal-perspective and high-likelihood hedges can preserve or increase persuasion ([Oba, Journal of Consumer Psychology](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jcpy.70026)). The corpus already agrees via Costello's calibrated confidence (behavior anchor 37 in section 01). This is why P2 passes calibrated uncertainty and the C3 lexicon targets weak hedges, not all uncertainty language.

## 7. Open questions

1. Does Wispr Flow's audio-on-disk carry timestamps precise enough to compute reply latency (C1)? Blocking check before the harness is built.
2. How much does Wispr's cleanup distort hedge and filler counts? Diff one real session's raw parakeet transcript against its chat text.
3. Should sessions 2 to 9 include micro-drills between conversations (Ultraspeaking-style 60-second games on the target behavior), or is the full-scenario rep the only unit? The games evidence suggests short isolated reps beat whole-task reps for a single behavior; the product currently only has whole sessions.
4. The proposed fix order (endings, leaks, then lead) assumes Ben's baseline shows those failures. The real session 1 record decides; the order in section 3 is a default, not a rule.
